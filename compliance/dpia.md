This is a deliberate lightweight assessment, not a formal GDPR Art. 35 DPIA. Screening result: the processing is not on AKI's Art. 35(4) mandatory-DPIA list and does not meet the EDPB WP248 "likely high risk" threshold at beta scale (no systematic monitoring, no large scale, no automated decisions with legal effect). We assess the two elevated-risk areas anyway because they touch children and health-adjacent data.

## Processing under assessment

Wobblepot plans family meals. The account-holding adult creates **household member profiles** — including children — containing names/labels, allergens, dietary restrictions, preferences, and macro targets. To generate meal plans, swaps, and preparation tips, this data is transmitted to **Anthropic, PBC** (US) as our processor (LLM inference). Full processor list, regions, and transfer safeguards: [`README.md`](./README.md); public disclosure at `/privacy/subprocessors`.

A household member can also connect an AI assistant, such as Claude, to the read-only MCP server (HON-1150, HON-1151). The assistant then reads household data on the member's instruction; risk area 3 assesses this. A member can also make a read-only API key for a screen or script (HON-1220), which risk area 3 covers too.

## Risk area 1 — children's data

**Model:** there are no child accounts. Member profiles are created and managed exclusively by the account-holding parent/guardian, who is the holder of parental responsibility and the one consenting. The privacy policy states: _"By adding a member under 16, you confirm that you are their parent or legal guardian and consent on their behalf."_ GDPR Art. 8 (child's own consent to an information society service) does not apply — the service is offered to the adult, not the child. The previously planned in-app acknowledgment checkbox (HON-467) was cancelled 2026-06-06 as evidentiary ceremony adding no legal basis; this DPIA reviewed and **concurs** with that decision.

**An MCP tool that calls an AI model — re-assessed 2026-10-19 (HON-1246):** `adjust_meal` sends the assistant's instruction, with the planned meal's ingredients, the household's pantry names and its dietary restrictions, to Anthropic, which turns it into ingredient changes the tool applies to that one entry. It is the same request the app's Adjust recipe makes (HON-1244), through the same code. **No new data category**: the meal, the pantry and the restrictions already reach Anthropic for Adjust recipe, the cook question and the preparation steps. **No new recipient**: Anthropic is already our processor for those features (`README.md`). The new input is the instruction text, which the household's own member typed to the assistant or confirmed, as they would type it in the app; it is not stored beyond the entry's `adjustmentNote`, which the app's own feature stores too. The tool needs `mcp:write`, so a read-only connection cannot call it. The spend is bounded by the `adjust-recipe` limit (30 calls an hour per household), the household's monthly AI cost cap and a 30-second budget per call, the same bounds as the app's feature, and each call is recorded in `ai_usage` against the household. The privacy policy says the instruction goes to our AI provider (`/privacy#ai-assistants`). Residual risk: low. Controller's decision: to be confirmed by the owner in review.

**Risks & mitigations**

| Risk                              | Mitigation                                                                                                                                      | Residual |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Excess data collected on children | Data minimisation: no DOB, no photos, no identifiers beyond a display name — not even an under-16 flag is stored, only what meal planning needs | Low      |
| Non-parent adds a child's data    | Household membership is invite-gated by the account holder; profiles are only visible inside the household                                      | Low      |
| Child's data outlives relevance   | 30-day account-deletion purge; 6 h backup (PITR) window; members deletable individually at any time                                             | Low      |

## Risk area 2 — AI processing via Anthropic

**What is sent:** household preferences, allergens, member dietary data, pantry context — embedded in generation prompts. **What is not sent:** email addresses, passwords, payment data. Anthropic processes as a processor under a DPA (filed: `dpas/anthropic-dpa-2026-06-02.pdf`) and **does not train models on the data**. Transfer basis: EU SCCs Module 2 + UK Addendum + EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

**Allergen data is the crux.** An allergy is a medical condition, so allergen fields are plausibly Art. 9 "data concerning health" — for _all_ members, adults included. Our position:

- Providing allergen data is **optional and user-initiated**, for the single, clearly stated purpose of meal planning; we treat that affirmative act, against the policy's explicit AI-processing disclosure, as Art. 9(2)(a) explicit consent.
- Necessity/proportionality: allergen-aware planning is the core safety feature of the product — the data cannot be omitted from prompts without defeating the user's purpose.
- Point-of-entry affirmation — **implemented (HON-666)**. The gap this assessment originally named was that the explicit-consent claim rested on the privacy policy alone. Allergens are entered once per household, for all members (adults and under-16s alike), in onboarding (step 3) and in the household settings form. Both render one component (`src/components/household/AllergenPicker.tsx`, HON-1082), so both show the same affirmation: directly under that checkbox group a notice states that ticked allergens are sent to our AI provider so meal plans avoid them, and links to the privacy policy. It is a passive notice rather than a checkbox: the affirmative act is ticking an allergen, and HON-467's cancellation reasoning (a consent checkbox is evidentiary ceremony) still holds.

**Risks & mitigations**

| Risk                                           | Mitigation                                                                                                                   | Residual   |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Health-adjacent data in a US processor's hands | DPA + no-training clause + SCCs M2 + UK Addendum + DPF; prompts carry no direct identifiers (no email/full account identity) | Low–medium |
| Art. 9 basis challenged                        | Optional provision + explicit policy disclosure + point-of-entry notice (HON-666); revisit on guidance                       | Low        |
| AI output leaking another household's data     | Stateless inference per request; no cross-household context is ever included in prompts                                      | Low        |

## Risk area 3 — connected AI assistants (MCP)

**What happens:** a household member connects an AI assistant to `/api/mcp`. The assistant registers as an OAuth client, the member signs in and allows it on `/consent`, and the assistant then calls tools on the member's request: read tools, and, if the member allowed the `mcp:write` scope, tools that update the shopping list (HON-1154). Technical detail: `docs/SECURITY.md` → OAuth for MCP clients, `docs/MCP_SERVER.md` → Threat model.

**Data the assistant receives:** with the `mcp:read` scope, the household's meal plan, shopping list, pantry and recipes (`get_meal_plan`, `get_shopping_list`, `get_pantry`, `list_meals`, `get_meal`). With the `profile` scope, the member's name and profile picture. No tool returns allergens or member profiles, but the household's allergens can be inferred: `list_meals` leaves out every meal that contains one (`loadMeals`), and its tool description says so, so one search per allergen that returns nothing tells the assistant the household avoids it. The privacy policy states this. Allergens are plausibly Art. 9 data (risk area 2), for children too. No read tool calls an AI model.

**Data the assistant can change (HON-1154):** only with the `mcp:write` scope, which the consent page lists as "Update your shopping list, pantry and meal plan, and add recipes." Today that is the shopping list and the pantry: `mark_purchased` (puts an ingredient in the pantry or takes a recent purchase out), `add_shopping_item`, `check_shopping_item` and `set_pantry_status` (records whether the household has an ingredient or ran out, HON-1173, HON-1201). In the meal plan, `swap_meal` replaces a planned meal with one that fits the household's diet, allergens and avoided ingredients, and `rate_meal` sets or clears the thumbs on a cooked meal, where the app shows them (HON-1174). `adjust_meal` changes the ingredients of one planned meal from an instruction ("no cream, use yoghurt"), through the app's Adjust recipe (HON-1246): it is the one tool that calls an AI model (see the 2026-10-19 re-assessment below). `create_meal` adds one of the household's own recipes (HON-1305): the member's recipe text, as the assistant passes it on, is stored as the member would type it in the app's recipe form. The server matches its ingredient names in the database and makes no model call, so the recipe reaches no new recipient and is no new data category: the household's own recipes are already stored and already readable through `get_meal`. The privacy policy names all of these. Every change is one the member can make, and undo, in the app: a recipe the assistant added is deleted on `/recipes`. No tool edits or deletes a recipe or changes members, preferences or allergens. A token with `mcp:read` only can call no write tool. No row records that an assistant made a change, because the models have no actor field; PostHog's `$mcp_tool_call` records that the tool was called, without its parameters.

**Data we store:** `oauth_client` (the registered app: name, redirect URIs; no personal data unless a user's id is attached), `oauth_consent` (which user allowed which client, with scopes and time), `oauth_access_token` and `oauth_refresh_token` (grants). PostHog receives `mcp:client_connected` when a member allows a client (client name, scopes, user and household id), and one `$mcp_tool_call` per tool call: the tool name, timing, error state and error message, the client's name, version and user agent, the parameter names, the user and household id, and the assistant's stated reason for the call. It never receives the parameter values or the data the tool returned. The consent page says each request is recorded before the member allows the client; the privacy policy lists the fields.

**Role of the assistant — decision 2026-10-11 (HON-1161):** the assistant is a **third party the user chooses, not a processor** of Honkadori OÜ. The member picks the client, allows it on `/consent` and instructs it; Honkadori OÜ does not choose the vendor, does not instruct it and does not decide what it does with the data. The disclosure is made on the member's instruction, as part of the service (Art. 6(1)(b)). The assistant's vendor is therefore not on the subprocessor list (`README.md`), and the privacy policy describes the disclosure and how the member stops it (`/privacy#ai-assistants`). Tool-call analytics rest on legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)): finding and fixing failing requests, disclosed on the consent page.

**Read-only API keys — added 2026-10-14 (HON-1220):** a member makes a key on `/profile` and gives it to a device they chose: a kitchen screen, a Home Assistant dashboard or their own script. The key reads `GET /api/v1/meal-plan` and `GET /api/v1/shopping-list`, which return the same data as `get_meal_plan` and `get_shopping_list`, and nothing else; it cannot write. The assessment is the same as for an assistant: the data goes on the member's instruction to a recipient the member chose (Art. 6(1)(b)), not to our processor. **No new data category** (the meal plan and shopping list are already in scope, and no allergen can be inferred from these two reads) and **no new recipient class** (a device or program the member controls, as with an assistant). We store `api_key`: the user, the name the member gave, the key's first 9 characters, its SHA-256 hash, when it was made, when it was last used, and when it expires, if its maker set an expiry (HON-1221). PostHog receives `api_key:created` and `api_key:revoked` (user and household id, key count), never a per-request event. Technical detail: `docs/SECURITY.md` → Read-only API keys, `docs/API.md`.

## Conclusion

Risk areas 1 and 2 are adequately mitigated at current scale; their one recommendation (allergen-entry affirmation, pre-public-launch) is implemented (HON-666). Risk area 3 is adequately mitigated: the controller decided on 2026-10-12 that the member's act of allowing an assistant, against the policy's explicit disclosure, is the Art. 9(2)(a) basis for allergens the assistant can infer. Re-assessed on 2026-10-13 for the `mcp:write` scope (HON-1154): writes add no new data category and no new recipient, and the new risk (an assistant changing data the member did not intend) is low, because the scope is opt-in on `/consent` and every change is reversible in the app. Re-assessed on 2026-10-14 for read-only API keys (HON-1220): same data as the MCP read tools, to a device the member chose, so no new data category and no new recipient class; the new risk (a leaked key) is low, because the key only reads and Revoke ends it at once. Re-assessed on 2026-10-19 for `adjust_meal`, the first MCP tool that calls an AI model (HON-1246): the same request, data and processor as the app's Adjust recipe, bounded by its rate limit, the cost cap and the per-call budget, so no new data category and no new recipient; residual risk low. Re-run this assessment on any review trigger above, before another MCP tool that calls an AI model with data Anthropic does not already receive, before one that deletes data the member cannot restore, and before an API key can write.
